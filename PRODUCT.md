# OpenCoK Product Definition

## Purpose and audience

OpenCoK lets communities create and manage independent Clash of Kings-compatible
kingdoms, with control over their rules, player experience and long-term continuity.
The product brings together kingdom management, familiar gameplay and room for
customization without depending on the original online service.

Community organizers need clear choices, dependable daily management and confidence
that player progress is protected. Players need reliable access, fair progression
and rules they can understand. Contributors need shared priorities and a clear view
of how their work improves the experience.

The starting experience includes two kingdoms. Each has its own players and progress,
with shared access to the wider community. Organizers can expand to additional
kingdoms as their communities grow.

The goal is a lasting product that communities can understand, manage and improve,
with clear expectations about what is available and how interruptions are resolved.

## Product promise

### Accessible and dependable kingdoms

- Help new organizers open a kingdom through clear guidance, without hidden steps
  or specialist knowledge.
- State which game editions are supported and what players can expect from each.
  The editions under consideration are 5.36.0, 6.12.20, 9.27.0 and 12.06.0;
  inclusion in this list is not a promise of support.
- Make supported editions usable throughout the core player journey, from entering
  the game to managing a city and taking part in kingdom activities.
- Keep essential play available when an optional feature is interrupted. Explain
  what is unavailable and what the player can do next.
- Preserve accounts, kingdom history, progress and personal images through routine
  maintenance and recovery. Resolve problems with as little disruption as possible.
- Give organizers practical guidance for opening, managing, expanding and restoring
  their kingdoms.

### Fair beginnings and meaningful progression

Accounts, buildings, research, heroes, equipment and events follow clear rules.
Every player starts from a consistent foundation and earns advancement through play.

- New players start at level 1 with a basic level-1 city, 100,000 wood,
  100,000 food and 1,000 gold. Stone, iron, silver and crystals start at zero.
  Faster progression options do not grant fully upgraded buildings, research,
  heroes or extra starting currency.
- Each account can have up to two characters per kingdom. Each character receives
  its starting package once, and returning to character creation never erases
  existing progress.
- Hua Mulan is an infantry combat hero with three branching talent trees and a
  target maximum level of 99. Talents have one or ten levels. Maximum percentage
  bonuses range from 50% to 500%; basic attributes and striking distance keep
  their stated values. Recruitment and talent learning use the usual fragments
  and points. Her portrait matches the close framing of neighboring heroes.
- Other heroes appear with white rarity and without a Legendary ribbon. Hua Mulan
  keeps her own rarity and ribbon. Visual changes preserve hero ownership,
  levels, talents and assignments.

### Personal expression and privacy

- Let players personalize their profiles with static images and animated GIFs.
- Make image size limits, eligibility and replacement rules clear. Profile images
  can normally be replaced once every three days, and a successful replacement
  removes the previous image while keeping a private confirmation.
- Keep profile customization responsive without interrupting browsing or play.
- Protect accounts and personal information. Players can change only what they
  are entitled to manage, and private information stays out of public reports.

### Clear status and support

Organizers can see whether their kingdoms are healthy, understand which activities
need attention and find unfinished or unusually slow actions. Players receive
useful explanations when something goes wrong and can distinguish an action that
is still waiting from one that has completed.

Support history helps resolve problems without exposing private player information.
Changes to resources, purchases and rewards must remain explainable through a
trustworthy account history.

## Gameplay experience

### Timed activities, production and rewards

- Construction, basic research, training, healing, fortifications, forging and
  workshop jobs have clear costs, deadlines and completion outcomes. Their
  progress survives interruptions. Workshop deliveries and waiting lists advance
  together, so players do not lose completed work or their place in line.
- City production accumulates up to a stated limit until collected. Collection
  adds the earned resources to the player's balance. Changes to production rates,
  VIP benefits, talents, temporary bonuses or equipment preserve what the player
  has already earned, including partial progress.
- Quest and sign-in rewards reflect actual progress and the relevant reward
  period. Repeating a claim never grants the same reward twice.
- Each exchange panel has clear offers, accepted tokens and daily refresh limits.
  Players see a current offer before spending, and daily limits renew once per day.
  Unclear historical exchanges are preserved until they can be resolved fairly.
- Activity rewards belong to the character that earned them and can be claimed
  once. The stated cost, reward, progression and any alliance contribution stay
  consistent. If a reward cannot be confirmed, the player keeps the right to
  claim it later.
- Rewards and experience follow a consistent order, honor earned bonuses and
  respect level limits. Repeating an action cannot create extra rewards or levels.

### City placement and shared gathering

- New cities, random relocations and returning lost cities follow the kingdom's
  established placement rules and require enough free space.
- Older kingdoms keep their existing cities. When placement rules are unclear,
  organizers must establish a verified policy before allowing new placements.
  Players are not charged for placements that cannot proceed, and existing
  progress is preserved.
- Multiple gathering parties can share a resource point without attacking one
  another or claiming exclusive ownership. Each party uses troops and companions
  the player owns, respects march limits and gathers its earned share of the
  remaining resources.
- Joining or leaving a resource point does not reduce another party's earned
  cargo. Parties return when a destination is full or unavailable.
- Cargo and extra rewards remain recoverable after an interruption. Returning
  parties receive their earned rewards once; repeated attempts cannot duplicate
  cargo, change an already determined reward or commit the same troops twice.
- Changes to gathering preserve previously completed journeys, remaining
  allowances and deliberately unavailable offers.

### Scouting and spending competitions

- Previewing a scouting mission costs nothing and does not commit an army.
  Departure uses the stated resources and the player's chosen troops and companions.
- Scouting reports reflect what the party could actually discover, respect
  protection rules and reach the correct player. Parties return without invented
  victories, casualties or loot. Interruptions do not cause duplicate charges or
  missing reports. Unavailable special targets produce a clear explanation.
- Spending rankings count confirmed spending accurately, including very large
  amounts. Previews, income and unverified historical totals do not count.
  Equal scores follow a consistent player order.
- Rankings respect the kingdom's visibility rules. Final prizes go to eligible
  participants after results close, and each prize can be received only once,
  including prizes delivered by mail.

## Mobile experience and future access

Keep the validated Android game experience available while improving the product.
Players should be able to discover updates, understand the steps needed to apply
them and continue with their existing accounts and progress. An update is complete
only when it is installed and usable.

Explore a renewed mobile experience gradually, without making it a condition for
current gameplay or promising a replacement date. Existing and future experiences
should preserve the same player identity and progress. Browser-based play remains
an exploratory direction with its own evaluation.

## Priorities and success criteria

1. **Make everyday play dependable:** resolve broken screens, visual issues,
   inaccessible events and inconsistent progression in supported game editions.
2. **Protect access to core activities:** reduce interruptions and clearly explain
   any unavailable optional features.
3. **Improve responsiveness and continuity:** shorten waits, prevent players from
   getting stuck and preserve ongoing activities through interruptions.
4. **Complete profile personalization:** make supported image formats and
   replacement rules reliable and easy to understand.
5. **Make kingdom management approachable:** give organizers clear guidance,
   support expectations and recovery options.

Success means a new organizer can open a kingdom using the available guidance;
players on supported editions can enter, build and complete core activities without
repeated long waits; and accounts and progress survive maintenance and interruptions.

Support commitments are clear for each game edition. Reported problems describe
the affected experience and the expected outcome. Each release explains what
changes, how it affects the community and what recovery options are available.
Private player information remains protected throughout.

## Boundaries

OpenCoK is independent and is not endorsed by the original developer, publisher or
other rights holders. Original game materials and trademarks remain subject to
their owners' terms. Contributors may share only work they own or are authorized
to share; the project license does not grant rights to third-party material.
Organizers and distributors are responsible for obtaining the necessary permissions.

The product focuses on independently managed communities. It does not promise to
recreate the publisher's worldwide service, offer public hosting for unrelated
communities or support every historical game edition. It does not claim ownership
of the original game or permission to redistribute material owned by others.

## Product decisions and collaboration

Keep community questions, proposals, accepted work, delivery priorities and lasting
guidance easy to find. Each accepted improvement needs a clear player or organizer
benefit, an owner and an observable definition of success.

Before committing to a change, establish who it helps, which game editions it
affects, what the experience should become and how existing progress will be
protected. When the impact is unclear, investigate it with the community before
making a commitment.
